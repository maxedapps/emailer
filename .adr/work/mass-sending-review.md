# Review: mass-sending plan and ADR-0011

> **Targets:** `.adr/work/mass-sending.md` (plan, "Ready for implementation"), `.adr/0011-open-recipient-set-and-paced-dispatch.md` (Proposed)
> **Reviewed:** 2026-09-15, read-only, against the installed `effect@4.0.0-rc.112`, `alchemy@2.0.0-beta.77`, `@distilled.cloud/aws@1.0.0-rc.9` and the current `apps/backend` sources.
> **Question asked:** implemented exactly as written with every named check green, how could the outcome still fail?

## Findings

### R1 — `cursor` is a DynamoDB reserved word; the checkpoint condition uses it unaliased — **High**

- **Where:** plan T2, `checkpoint(id, runToken, previous, next)`: `#state = :sending AND runToken = :run AND (attribute_not_exists(cursor) OR cursor = :previous)`; also `completeRun` ("removes `cursor`") and META shape `cursor?`.
- **Evidence:** DynamoDB reserved-word list contains `CURSOR` (docs.aws.amazon.com/amazondynamodb/latest/developerguide/ReservedWords.html; the list is case-insensitive). The plan aliases `count` and `state` only (Research bullet "Store contract"; T2). `TEXT` is reserved too, but `text` only appears in `Put` items, never in an expression, so it is harmless.
- **Realistic failure:** every `checkpoint`, `completeRun` and `pauseRun` request is rejected with `ValidationException` at the first page boundary. The unit suite cannot see it: `scriptedTable` evaluates no expressions (`Storage/Testing.ts:55-119`), and T2's tests pin the expression text the implementer wrote. Live, the failure is `unavailable` → `reportedAndFatal` → the invocation dies; SQS redelivers after the 30-minute visibility timeout, five times, then dead-letters (`AWS/Lambda/QueueEventSource.ts:75-77`; Lambda SQS error handling). The T9 gate would fail, but only after 2.5 hours of `sending`, and the plan's risk note points the implementer at the limiter item rather than the expression.
- **Correction:** alias every expression use: `#cursor` in `ConditionExpression`, `UpdateExpression` (`SET #cursor = :next`, `REMOVE #cursor`). Add to T2's test list an assertion that no `ConditionExpression`/`UpdateExpression` in any campaign request contains a bare reserved word (a one-line check over the recorded requests against the reserved list, or simply over `{count, state, cursor, text}`).

### R2 — Three throttled attempts then `rejected` can burn an entire audience — **High**

- **Where:** plan T5 outcomes: "rejected `rate-limited` → up to three more attempts, each after `RateLimiter.sleep`, then settle rejected"; ADR-0011 Decision "Throttle is a retry, not a death".
- **Evidence:** the limiter only sees this stage's sends; the ADR itself says the account rate is "shared with another workload in this account" (Context) and the 20 % margin "covers the other workload" (Decision). `TooManyRequestsException` and `LimitExceededException` map to `rate-limited` (`Mailer.ts:44-46`). A `rate-limited` row is settled `rejected` and "never re-attempted" (ADR Decision, per-recipient rows). Re-entering the limiter after a throttle yields ~0 delay for a lone runner (`RateLimiter.ts:156-160`: the delay is relative to this runner's own slot, not to SES's rejection), so the three retries land within about a second.
- **Realistic failure:** the other workload (a bulk mailer) sends a campaign while ours is running. SES throttles for tens of seconds. Every recipient the dispatcher reaches in that interval takes four throttled attempts in ~1-2 s and is settled `rejected` — at 11/s that is hundreds of recipients permanently lost per minute of contention, with the campaign reporting `completed`. "Campaigns of any size worked through" fails silently; nothing in T9 can reproduce it (the simulator does not contend).
- **Correction (smallest):** treat exhausted throttle retries exactly like `sending-paused`: settle that one recipient `rejected` (or, since a throttle provably never sent, leave it and re-claim later — but that breaks the never-resubmit invariant, so `rejected` is the honest choice), then `pauseRun("rate-limited")` and return; the operator resumes. Space the retries with a real backoff (`Effect.sleep` 1 s, 2 s, 4 s in addition to the limiter) so a short burst does not pause at all. Add `"rate-limited"` to the `paused.reason` literal in T1.

### R3 — The T9 spread assertion is a false green — **Medium**

- **Where:** plan T9 (`finishedAt` spread ≥ `59 / limit × 0.8` s, `limit` from `aws sesv2 get-account`); Final acceptance "spread at or below the account rate with margin"; ADR Confirmation.
- **Evidence:** the dispatcher sends sequentially with one `SendEmail` round trip (~100-250 ms) plus a 2-action `TransactWriteItems` per recipient. Sixty sequential recipients therefore spread over ≥ 6-15 s with **no limiter at all**. The threshold at `MaxSendRate = 14` is 3.4 s; at 50 it is 0.94 s. The formula also uses the raw `MaxSendRate` where the dispatcher uses `floor(MaxSendRate × 0.8)`, so it is looser than the design on top.
- **Realistic failure:** a dispatcher that never calls the limiter (wrong Layer, `sleep` short-circuited, store returning zero) passes the gate; the ADR then records "pacing proven live" on no evidence.
- **Correction:** replace the spread with two assertions that can actually fail: (a) read `RATELIMIT#ses-send` after the run and assert `count === submissions` (60 plus any recorded `rate-limited` retries) — proves every submission consulted the shared store; (b) bucket accepted rows by `finishedAt` second and assert no bucket exceeds `floor(MaxSendRate × 0.8)` — the ceiling, which is the property that matters. The sleep arithmetic stays proven by the T3 `TestClock` test.

### R4 — The continuation path, which is what makes "any size" true, is never exercised live — **Medium**

- **Where:** plan T5 ("deadline near → enqueue continuation"), T9 Tests ("not forced live"), Deferrals.
- **Evidence:** at 11/s the 60-recipient gate finishes in ~6 s inside one invocation; the dispatcher's `sqs:SendMessage` grant, the `Dispatch` message round trip through a real SQS record, and `beginRun` on a mid-cursor campaign are all reached only by campaigns longer than ~5 minutes — i.e. first in production.
- **Realistic failure:** an IAM or encoding mistake in the continuation surfaces as a large campaign stuck `sending` after its first slice, retried five times at 30-minute intervals, then dead-lettered.
- **Correction (also a simplification):** make the continuation the only way to the next page: after every page's won checkpoint, enqueue `{campaignId, runToken}` and return; never loop pages in-process. One extra SQS hop per 50 recipients (sub-second) is negligible against 4.5 s of paced sending per page, the in-process page loop and its "deadline near → continue vs enqueue" branch disappear, and the two-page live gate exercises the grant, the message schema and the mid-campaign `beginRun` on every run. The in-page deadline check stays (R8).

### R5 — Rows claimed by a crashed slice are neither settled nor counted; ADR says otherwise — **Medium**

- **Where:** ADR-0011 Consequences: "claimed-but-unsettled rows from the crashed slice stay `uncertain` and are never resent"; plan T2 SEND# states `unconfirmed|accepted|rejected|uncertain|skipped`; outcome "exactly one settled row per recipient".
- **Evidence:** nothing in T2/T5 transitions `unconfirmed` → anything after a crash (a Lambda timeout inside the 8 s `submissionTimeout`, an OOM). The redelivered slice gets `already-claimed` and continues. The `uncertain` counter is incremented only by `settleRecipient` on a `SubmissionUncertain`. So after a crash the campaign completes with `accepted + rejected + uncertain + skipped < members` and no field explains the gap.
- **Correction:** either (a) correct the ADR and the plan's end state: such rows stay `unconfirmed`, are visible only in `SEND#` rows, and are the accepted residual; or (b) make the redelivered runner reconcile: on `already-claimed`, if the row is `unconfirmed` and `startedAt` is older than the queue's visibility timeout, settle it `uncertain` (the same transaction as today with the condition `#state = :unconfirmed AND startedAt < :cutoff`). (b) is one extra branch and keeps the counters exact; (a) is fine if the user accepts the gap. Do not leave the ADR asserting (b) while the plan builds (a).

### R6 — The limiter item is per stage, not per account — **Medium**

- **Where:** ADR-0011 Decision "one key for the account and Region"; Consequences "A separate production account would need its own limiter item, which the table-per-stack design gives for free"; plan T3 (item in "the existing table").
- **Evidence:** the data table is per stage (README teardown inventory; `Storage/Table.ts`), so `RATELIMIT#ses-send` in `test-b` and in `prod` are different items sharing one SES rate. The deliverability lane runs `test-a` concurrently (`.adr/work/deliverability.md:6`).
- **Realistic failure:** two stages sending at once in one account each pace at 80 % of the full rate → 160 % → throttles → R2. Today the risk is one message from `test-a`; it becomes real the day a second long-lived stage exists.
- **Correction:** state the limitation honestly in the ADR (the limiter coordinates the runners of one stage; only one stage may send at volume per account) and reverse the misleading consequence sentence. No code change now.

### R7 — `beginRun` does not return what the loop needs; the undefined-cursor condition is unspecified — **Medium**

- **Where:** plan T2 `beginRun(...) → running | stale`; T5 "Loop pages of `listMembers(listId, 50, cursor)`", `checkpoint(prev, ...)`.
- **Evidence:** the dispatcher receives only `{campaignId, runToken}`; `listId`, `subject`, `text` and the stored `cursor` (which is the first page's `previous`) come from META, and the plan names no read. `checkpoint`'s `cursor = :previous` cannot bind `:previous` to `undefined` on the first page after a fresh run, so the condition needs two shapes (`attribute_not_exists(#cursor)` alone, or `#cursor = :previous`), and the deadline-before-first-member case (`lastProcessed === previous`) must not `SET #cursor` to nothing.
- **Correction:** `beginRun` is an `UpdateItem` with `ReturnValues: ALL_NEW`; return `{ outcome: "running", campaign: <decoded META> } | "stale"` and read nothing else. Specify both condition shapes and the no-progress checkpoint (`won` without an update, or skip the checkpoint and just return — the message is redelivered anyway if nothing was enqueued; simplest: enqueue only if `lastProcessed !== previous`, otherwise return and let SQS redeliver).

### R8 — The deadline check runs before a sleep of unknown length; `SendMessage` failure handling is unstated — **Medium**

- **Where:** plan T5 per-member order: deadline check → … → `RateLimiter.sleep` → `mailer.submit`.
- **Evidence:** `sleep` computes and performs the delay internally (`RateLimiter.ts:355-364`); with N runners the delay is ≈ `N / limit` s (Research). A check that passes with 31 s left, followed by a 20 s sleep, an 8 s submit and a 5 s settle, ends inside Lambda's kill window → an `unconfirmed` row (R5). Separately, "every storage failure dies through `reportedAndFatal`" does not cover a failed `SendMessage` after a won checkpoint; if it were caught and swallowed the campaign would sit `sending` with no message in flight until an operator noticed.
- **Correction:** call `limiter.consume({ onExceeded: "delay", ... })`, compare `delay + submissionTimeout + 2 × operationTimeout` with the remaining budget, and only then `Effect.sleep(delay)` (a consumed-but-unused slot is harmless). State that a `SendMessage` failure after a won checkpoint dies like a storage failure so the message is redelivered and the run resumes from the persisted cursor.

### R9 — The API keeps the mailer and its `ses:SendEmail` grant; its own enqueue failure has no mapping — **Medium**

- **Where:** plan T6 (`Api.ts`: adds `SendMessage`, deletes `publiclySent`; nothing about `MailerLive`); T6 tests "the API never calls the mailer".
- **Evidence:** `Api.ts:166-185` provides `MailerLive`, whose Layer binds `AWS.SES.SendEmail` and grants it (`Mailer.ts:185`). After T6 nothing in the API uses it. ADR-0008 makes per-host IAM the point of the capability split. And the `send` error list becomes `BadRequestNoContent, NotFound, StorageUnavailable` (T1) while `SendMessage` can fail after `enqueueCampaign` committed; the plan says only that `send` on `queued` re-sends the wake-up.
- **Correction:** remove `Mailer`/`MailerLive` from `Api.ts` (the API's role then loses `ses:SendEmail`; add that to the T9 IAM expectations). Map a `SendMessage` failure in `send`/`resume` to `StorageUnavailable({ operationId: "dispatch" })` after logging, so the CLI's existing nonzero-and-retry contract covers it and the retry re-sends the wake-up.

### R10 — Scope not needed for the outcome — **Medium (simplicity)**

- **T8 transient-bounce escalation:** unrelated to dispatching or pacing, adds `UpdateItem` to the feedback consumer's IAM (the ADR-0008 boundary the cleanup just narrowed), a new item type and a threshold policy. Cut from this lane; it is its own two-hour slice with its own record.
- **Feedback counts in `campaigns get`:** `readCampaignFeedback` pages the whole `FEEDBACK#` prefix on every read (`Storage/Feedback.ts:126-157`); for a large campaign that is an unbounded query behind a 60 s API call to answer two numbers nobody asked for in the outcome. Cut, or defer to the reputation lane where the numbers have a consumer.
- **`CampaignNotPaused` + 409:** `send` already returns the campaign unchanged for states it cannot act on; `resume` can do the same (return the campaign; the CLI prints it). One fewer error class, route error, status-table case and CLI branch.
- **CLI `requestTimeout` to 30 s:** no request ran a send before either (the 70 s was sized to the API's 60 s); leave it.
- **`ReturnValuesOnConditionCheckFailure` in the store:** not needed. Condition the reset on `#expiresAt <= :now` instead of on the observed value: `SET #count = :tokens, #expiresAt = :nowPlusExtend` with `ConditionExpression: attribute_exists(pk) AND #expiresAt <= :now`. Any concurrent extend or reset makes it fail, after which the common path is retried. That removes the failed-item decode, the "unexpected shape" fallback in T3's risk note, and the dependency on how `@distilled.cloud/aws` surfaces `Item` on `ConditionalCheckFailedException` (typed `any`, `dynamodb.d.ts:24-33`). Keep: per-recipient skip rows (they are what makes skip counts idempotent), the `uncertain` counter, the `paused.reason` field.

### R11 — Cross-lane overlaps the merge notes miss — **Low**

- **Where:** plan Handoff "Shares `README.md` … and nothing else"; header "Does not touch … the ADRs 0002/0009/0010 bodies".
- **Evidence:** this lane edits `wiki/aws/ses.md` (T10) and ADR-0002's header (T10 lifecycle line at `:36`); the deliverability lane edits `wiki/aws/ses.md:105` and ADR-0002's header too (`.adr/work/deliverability.md:40, 126-128`, T4). Different lines, but three shared files, not one. Also, deliverability T3 sends through "today's single-recipient send path" and expects `submission.state: "accepted"` and exit 0 (`deliverability.md:114`); if this lane merges first, that step must be re-read (send returns `queued`; the gate must poll and read the delivered message's headers after `completed`).
- **Correction:** list all three shared files; add a one-line sequencing note to both plans.

### R12 — Simulator-only is purely procedural; two cheap guards are missing — **Low**

- **Where:** plan T9 `IntegrationSupport.ts`; T6 `.env.example`.
- **Evidence:** after T6 nothing reads `EMAILER_ALLOWED_RECIPIENTS`, but `.env.test` still carries it with a real mailbox first; `uniqueAddress` mints `probe-<id>@example.invalid` (`IntegrationSupport.ts:217`) and nothing stops a future test from adding such a contact to a list and sending. A reused stage table is not a risk by itself (a campaign mails only its list's members), the fresh `test-b` table even less.
- **Correction:** in `IntegrationSupport`, one helper `sendToSimulatorList(client, listId, campaignId)` that pages `listMembers` and throws if any member address does not end with `@simulator.amazonses.com` before calling `send`; use it everywhere the suite sends. Delete the key from the local `.env.test` as part of T6. Both are test-side; no product guard is reintroduced.

### R13 — Clerical gaps in T9 — **Low**

- Paths: the live suites are `apps/backend/src/*.integration.test.ts`, not `apps/backend/test/*.integration.test.ts` (only `IntegrationSupport.ts` lives under `test/`).
- `awaitCampaignState` needs a timeout derived from `members / floor(MaxSendRate × 0.8)` plus polling slack; an account with `MaxSendRate = 1` takes 60 s for the flow plus ~15 s for the other campaigns, inside the 120 s test timeout but not by much. Size it from the quota read at test start.
- The concurrent-sends case should assert rows, not counters: a losing duplicate runner may still be settling a row it claimed when the winner writes `completed`, so `progress.accepted` can lag the row states for a few seconds (T2 `settleRecipient` is deliberately not conditioned on the run token or state).
- Sixty contacts at `maxImportEntries = 20` (`Schemas.ts:19`) is three imports, as stated; 60 > 50 crosses one page boundary. Fine.

### R14 — ADR-0011 statements the plan does not deliver — **Low**

- "Every transition is a conditional write on state and run token, so a stale runner after a resume cannot write": `settleRecipient` is conditioned on the SEND# row and `attribute_exists(pk)` only (T2), by design, so a stale slice does write its own settles and counter increments. Say so; it is correct behaviour.
- "Runners equal active campaigns by construction": duplicate deliveries and `send`-on-`queued` re-sends make extra runners; the claim is "at least one runner per active campaign, usually one".
- "A crash mid-page delays the campaign by the visibility timeout": every failed invocation does — a throttled `TransactWriteItems`, a `corrupt` item — and a deterministic failure dead-letters after five receives with the campaign stuck in `sending`, which `resume` cannot reach (`paused` only). Recovery is a DLQ redrive of the same message; record that, and add it to the README's replay section.
- "claimed-but-unsettled rows … stay `uncertain`": see R5.
- Authority line attributes the standard-queue choice to the user; the alternatives list rejects FIFO on technical grounds. If the user only decided the limiter/store, say "proposed here" for the queue type as ADR-0003 does for its transport.

## Verified claims

- `RateLimiter.sleep` forces `onExceeded: "delay"`; `consume` passes `limit: undefined` to `store.fixedWindow` in delay mode and computes the delay as `floor((count − 1) / limit) × window − elapsed` (`RateLimiter.ts:97-160, 355-364`). The plan's reading is right.
- Store contract and the memory algorithm (absent or expired → `count = 0, expiresAt = now`; add tokens; extend by `refill × tokens`; return `[count, expiresAt − now]`) match `RateLimiter.ts:618-627, 721-737`; the Redis script's `max(1, ceil(refillms × tokens))` matches the plan's `extend` (`:1019-1047`). A single `UpdateItem` with `if_not_exists` arithmetic and the condition `attribute_not_exists(pk) OR #expiresAt > :now` reproduces the common path; the expired path needs a second write (R10 gives the simpler condition).
- `UpdateItemInput.ReturnValuesOnConditionCheckFailure` exists and Alchemy's `UpdateItemRequest = Omit<UpdateItemInput, "TableName">` passes it through (`dynamodb.d.ts:2375-2400`, `AWS/DynamoDB/UpdateItem.ts:6-9`); `ConditionalCheckFailedException` carries `Item: any` (`dynamodb.d.ts:24-33`).
- `SendQuota` fields are all optional (`sesv2.d.ts:865-869`); `GetAccount()` is an argument-less binding (`AWS/SES/GetAccount.ts:27-36`).
- `consumeQueueMessages(queue, { batchSize: 1 }, process)` forwards `batchSize`, grants receive/delete/get-attributes, and runs `process` under `Effect.orDie` (`AWS/SQS/QueueEventSource.ts:63-73`, `AWS/Lambda/QueueEventSource.ts:44-77`); a failed invocation makes the message visible again after the visibility timeout, and `maxReceiveCount` then dead-letters it.
- `SendMessage(queue)` from the consuming function is a second grant on the same ARN via `makeQueueUrlHttpBinding` (`AWS/SQS/BindingHttp.ts:43-71`); no self-reference.
- `HandlerContext` is provided per invocation (`AWS/Lambda/Function.ts:78-81, 1052-1058`).
- Queue props `visibilityTimeout`, `redrivePolicy { deadLetterTargetArn, maxReceiveCount }`, `messageRetentionPeriod` exist (`AWS/SQS/Queue.ts:57-85`); 30 min = 6 × 5 min and DLQ 14 d > 4 d satisfy `wiki/aws/sqs.md:35-40, 62`.
- `Config` reads in a Function constructor are deploy-time captures (`wiki/alchemy/runtime-and-bindings.md:23-27`); an optional `EMAILER_DAILY_SEND_CEILING` read there is fine.
- Leaf-module rule (`ADR-0004:47`): `Dispatch.ts` without a handler, imported by both functions, is the right shape.
- T2 transactions: `claimRecipient` (ConditionCheck META + Put SEND#), `skipRecipient` (Put SEND# + Update META), `settleRecipient` (Update SEND# + Update META) each touch two distinct items; none targets one item twice. Skip and settle counters are exact because both are gated by a row condition that a duplicate cannot pass.
- Duplicate-runner trace (two deliveries of one message): both pass `beginRun` (`queued OR sending` with the same token is the correct condition for a duplicate and for a post-crash redelivery); every recipient is claimed by exactly one; the second `checkpoint(prev, next)` fails on `cursor = :previous`; the loser sends at most the remainder of its current page, every send claim-protected and limiter-paced; only the checkpoint winner enqueues, so one continuation chain. After `pauseRun`, the loser's next claim fails on `#state = :sending` (`stale`); after `resume` it fails on the run token; its already-claimed rows are settled by it, correctly. No double submission, no double count.
- `listMembers` cursor: `ExclusiveStartKey = memberKey(listId, contactId)` (`Storage/Membership.ts:229-234`); a contact id that no longer has a member item is still a valid start position for a `Query`. Page order: `localeCompare` on lowercase UUID v4 agrees with DynamoDB's byte order (checked on sample ids), so "cursor = last processed contact id" resumes correctly. A contact dropped by hydration is simply not mailed; the cursor still advances from `LastEvaluatedKey`.
- Deleted-operation grep: `readAudience`, `audienceProbeLimit`, `claimCampaign`, `finalizeCampaign`, `MembershipConflict`, `InvalidAudience`, `SendUnconfirmed`, `SendStatusUnrecorded`, `publiclySent`, `SendFailure`, `allowedRecipients`, `SendNotAccepted` are referenced only in the files T1/T2/T6/T7/T9 rewrite (`Campaigns.ts`, `Diagnostics.ts`, `Api.ts`, `Mailer.ts`, `Storage/Campaigns.ts`, `Storage/Membership.ts`, `Storage/Testing.ts:174`, `IntegrationSupport.ts`, the CLI `Commands.ts`/`Diagnostics.ts`, their tests, `packages/api`). ADR-0005's three mentions of `claimCampaign` are historical prose and need no edit beyond the planned lifecycle line.
- Simulator labels: AWS documents "the mailbox simulator supports labeling … bounce+label1@simulator.amazonses.com" and that labeling works with any simulator address; `wiki/aws/ses.md:65-67` records a labelled `complaint+` observed working on 2026-09-11. `bounce+<runId>@` is safe to assume.
- `wiki/aws/ses.md:55`: simulator sends are rate-limited and billed but do not count toward the daily quota — so `dailyExhausted` is unit-only, as the plan implies.
- The 0.8 margin is a reasonable guess for an unknowable co-tenant rate; with R2 in place it stops being load-bearing.
- Quota changes between runs skew one window's delay arithmetic (the store persists `count`/`expiresAt` under the old `refillRate`); transient and bounded by one idle reset, not worth handling.

## Verdict

**Changes required.** R1 breaks every campaign longer than one page and is invisible to the unit gate; R2 lets a co-tenant's burst silently reject an audience while the campaign reports `completed`. R3-R9 are correctness and evidential gaps that the plan's own checks would not surface; R10 removes work the outcome does not need. None changes the architecture: the storage model, the limiter, the queue and the per-recipient rows stand.

### Fix-caused finding

#### R15 — The R3 replacement assertion `count === submissions` is a false red — **S2 / C3**

- **Where:** plan T9 (:212 "the `RATELIMIT#ses-send` item's `count` equals the number of submissions"; :227 risk note "If the limiter item's count is short, the dispatcher is not consulting the store"); Final acceptance (:249 "the limiter item's count equal to submissions"); ADR-0011 Confirmation ("the limiter item's count equals the number of submissions"). Caused by the previous round's R3 correction, which this round retracts.
- **Evidence:** the store contract the plan copies (Research :71, T3 :128) is the memory store's: `if (!counter || counter.expiresAt <= now) { count: 0, expiresAt: now }`, then `count += tokens; expiresAt += refillRateMillis × tokens` (`node_modules/effect/src/unstable/persistence/RateLimiter.ts:721-737`; the Redis script at `:1019-1047` is the same). After one token the item's TTL is one refill interval, `1000 / limit` ms — about 91 ms at `MaxSendRate = 14` (limit 11). The dispatcher sends sequentially: one `SendEmail` round trip plus one `TransactWriteItems` per recipient, 150-300 ms. Every consume therefore finds the item expired, resets it, and returns `count = 1` (correctly, with delay 0: `windowNumber = floor(0 / 11) = 0`). `count` accumulates only while consumes arrive faster than the refill interval, i.e. only when the limiter is actually binding, and even then the continuation hop between pages can outlast the TTL. The plan's Research :70 sentence "resets only after an idle gap longer than the remaining TTL" is literally true and is exactly what misled the R3 remedy: the remaining TTL after one token is one refill interval, not one window.
- **Realistic failure:** the live gate on `test-b` fails on a correct implementation (`count` is 1, or a small burst size, never 60), blocking T9 and acceptance; the risk note then sends the implementer to "check the Layer wiring", and the nearest way to make the number come out is to change the store's reset semantics, which would break the delay arithmetic the limiter depends on.
- **Correction (wording only, three places):** in T9 and the ADR Confirmation replace the count assertion with "the `RATELIMIT#ses-send` item exists and its `expiresAt` is at or after the run's start" (proves the store was written during this run, which is what R3 wanted: an unwired limiter cannot pass), keep the per-second ceiling bucket, and state plainly that at typical quotas the sequential pace never approaches the limit, so the live gate proves wiring and the ceiling while the pacing arithmetic stays proven by the T3 store test and the T5 fake-limiter test. Reword the T9 risk note to "if the item is absent, the dispatcher is not consulting the store", and align T10's "what local checks prove" line. Optionally fix Research :70 to say "resets whenever a refill interval passes between consumes".
