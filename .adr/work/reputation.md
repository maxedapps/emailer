## Outcome and boundaries

- **Problem and target:** the service suppresses bounces and complaints but reads no reputation signal, notifies nobody, never counts feedback per campaign, never escalates transient bounces, and gives an operator no way to inspect or clear an address. Target: every alarm reaches a person; the dispatcher stops on alarm or enforcement; a campaign with a bad list pauses itself within its first few hundred sends; addresses that keep soft-bouncing are skipped for a window; delivery delays are visible in the logs with their type; an operator can see and clear an address from the CLI.
- **In scope:** alert topic and email subscription; alarm actions on all alarms; four reputation alarms and set-level reputation metrics; `DELIVERY_DELAY` publishing and a typed log line; campaign counters `bounced` and `complained` written by the feedback consumer and exposed in `campaigns get`; per-run circuit breaker; alarm and enforcement gate in the dispatcher; transient-bounce window in `addressStatus`; bounded retry of transaction conflicts in the storage primitives; `addresses status` and `addresses unsuppress` through the API and CLI; ADR-0012; README, wiki and `.env.example`.
- **Out of scope:** SES-side pausing of the configuration set or account; SES tenants; Virtual Deliverability Manager; Google Postmaster Tools automation and DMARC report parsing (manual weekly duties, documented); storing or counting delivery delays; a topic access policy (deferred until a notification is observed to fail); resubscribing an opted-out address; automatic un-suppression; changing the daily ceiling or pacing; Slack or other channels.
- **Approach:** one leaf module `Reputation.ts` owns the topic and the four alarms so the stack and the dispatcher yield the same resources, and it declares nothing that would run at a cold start. The dispatcher's existing budget read grows into a guard: quota, alarm state and enforcement status in one service. The feedback consumer's per-recipient write becomes one transaction per event that also moves the campaign counter; because two writers now touch the campaign record, the transaction primitive retries conflict cancellations, which provably applied nothing. Address state stays derived from three rows read in one consistent batch, and the operator operations join the audience capability that already holds the needed bindings. Everything is exercised by unit suites; the live gate proves the alarm path with `SetAlarmState`, the breaker with a lopsided labelled campaign, and the operator commands against a seeded account-list entry.

## Research

- **Alchemy fights a runtime pause.** `ConfigurationSet` reconcile compares the observed `SendingEnabled` with `news.sendingEnabled ?? true` and puts it back on any deploy that changes the set's props, after adoption, or under `--force`. A guardian that disables the set through the API would be undone by the deploy that ships the next feature. Alarm state read by the dispatcher has no such conflict.
- **`DescribeAlarms` is a first-class binding.** `AWS.CloudWatch.DescribeAlarms(...alarms)` resolves the alarm names from the resources' outputs at runtime and registers the grant at construction, exactly like the SES and DynamoDB bindings the functions already use. A module-level resource yielded from both the stack and a function registers once (precedent: `dispatchFailures` in `Dispatch.ts`).
- **Props run at cold start.** The dispatcher yields the alarms in its constructor; a resource's props effect is evaluated wherever its fully qualified name is first registered, and the Lambda bootstrap provides no `AWSEnvironment`. That is the death ADR-0003 recorded for the event destination. The topic therefore takes no props, and the alarms read only outputs (`configurationSet.configurationSetName`, `topic.topicArn`).
- **Same-account alarm publishing.** SNS's default topic policy admits publishers from the owning account, and CloudWatch publishes with the source-account context; the explicit `cloudwatch.amazonaws.com` statement in AWS's notification page is the cross-account and confused-deputy hardening. The gate observes the `ALARM:` mail; if it does not arrive, the statement is added with that failure as the evidence, through a deploy-side mechanism that does not put a props effect on the dispatcher's path.
- **Email subscriptions.** `Subscribe` with `returnSubscriptionArn: true` (Alchemy's default) returns a real ARN while unconfirmed, so `pendingConfirmation` is `false` immediately; SNS deletes an unconfirmed subscription after 48 hours, after which the next deploy re-subscribes. Destroy swallows NotFound. The gate confirms first, so a pending entry's fate on `Unsubscribe` does not matter. The confirmation mail's `SubscribeURL` is followed from the operator mailbox with the CLI's message read.
- **Set-level reputation metrics.** `PutConfigurationSetReputationOptions` makes `Reputation.BounceRate` and `Reputation.ComplaintRate` appear under the dimension `ses:configuration-set` with the set name as value, after the set has sent. AWS documents no period; the metrics move about daily. Simulator sends and sends to suppressed addresses are excluded, so the alarm path is tested with `SetAlarmState`, asserted `ALARM` right before the send, and reset afterwards.
- **Two writers on one item.** With the feedback consumer adding counters to the campaign record while the dispatcher settles rows against it, DynamoDB cancels the loser of a simultaneous transaction with reason `TransactionConflict`, and refuses a single-item write with `TransactionConflictException`. Both mean nothing was applied, unlike a lost response, so retrying them is safe and bounded retries belong in the primitive, above the client-level opt-out.
- **Delivery delay events.** Detail-type `Email Delivery Delayed`, `eventType: "DeliveryDelay"`, `deliveryDelay: { delayType, delayedRecipients[{ emailAddress, status, diagnosticCode }], expirationTime, reportingMTA, timestamp }`. `SpamDetected` and `IPFailure` are reputation signals; `MailboxFull` is not. The user chose a typed log line over storage.
- **A paused configuration set is indistinguishable in v2.** `SendEmail` has no set-specific exception; both cases surface as `SendingPausedException`. Irrelevant now that nothing pauses the set, but recorded in the wiki.
- **Breaker sample sizes and timing.** At 0.1 percent one complaint in fewer than 1,000 sends already breaches, at 5 percent one bounce in fewer than 20 does; AWS publishes no per-campaign rule. Minimums of 200 accepted for bounces and 1,000 for complaints keep single events from pausing a campaign. The breaker evaluates only at slice start, one page of fifty per slice, so a live proof needs enough pages after the minimum and enough bounce feedback already recorded: 400 members with half bouncing gives four evaluations past the minimum and needs only ten recorded bounces at the first of them.
- **Transient bounces.** SES emits a `Transient` bounce only after it has stopped retrying (up to 14 hours). Industry rules range from two consecutive over two weeks to seven; the user chose three within thirty days. Delivery events are not published, so the window is time-based and derived at read time. `Undetermined` bounces join the window, as they are "recorded without suppressing" today.
- **Suppression echoes.** A bounce with subtype `OnAccountSuppressionList` or `Suppressed`, or a complaint with `complaintSubType: OnAccountSuppressionList`, means SES accepted a send it never delivered because the address was already listed. They suppress and record but do not count toward the breaker: nothing reached a mailbox, and SES excludes them from its own rates.
- **Simulator bounces and the account list.** The wiki records that `bounce+` simulator addresses are not added to the account suppression list. The un-suppress case therefore seeds one labelled address with `PutSuppressedDestination` from the test's own credentials and deletes it in a `finally`.
- **operator mailbox use.** The gate uses the inbox twice: once to read the SNS confirmation mail and follow its link, once to observe one alarm notification. Both are header-and-body checks, not recipients of campaign mail.
