# ADR-0012: Reputation guardrails: alerts, alarm-gated dispatch, a campaign circuit breaker and a transient-bounce window

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-09-15
- Accepted: 2026-09-15
- Authority: The user asked on 2026-09-15 for the reputation lane as one plan covering both phases, "using Alchemy and Effect properly, according to their philosophies and best practices, using their full APIs", and chose five design points the same day: the dispatcher reads alarm state instead of an SES-side pause, a stack-owned alert topic per stage, the campaign breaker at AWS's review ratios with minimum samples, a transient-bounce window of three in thirty days, and delivery delays logged with their type rather than stored. Acceptance follows the live gate in [the plan](work/reputation.md).
- Supersedes in part: [ADR-0003](0003-feedback-events-through-eventbridge.md)'s "neither Lambda calls any SES suppression API"; [ADR-0008](0008-storage-capabilities-and-error-boundaries.md)'s capability rows for `FeedbackStore` and `AudienceStore`; [ADR-0011](0011-open-recipient-set-and-paced-dispatch.md)'s three pause reasons and its deferral of feedback counts to this lane.
- Superseded in part: [ADR-0013](0013-repeat-safe-writes.md) for "Transaction conflicts are retried, lost responses are not" and its client-level opt-out.

## Context

Bounces and complaints are consumed and suppressed, and campaigns pause on quota, throttling and an SES-side pause. Nothing reads a reputation signal, no alarm has an action, feedback rows are never counted, transient bounces never escalate, and an operator has no way to see or clear an address's state. The SES account and its suppression list are shared with any other SES sender on the account; the account's enforcement status is Healthy at about 0.02 percent bounces and 0.01 percent complaints, all from that other workload.

Four facts from the installed Alchemy 2.0.0-beta.77 and the SES documentation shape the design. Alchemy's `ConfigurationSet` reconcile re-asserts `sendingEnabled` on every deploy that touches the set or runs with `--force`, so a set paused through the SES API is silently re-enabled by infrastructure. Alchemy has no binding for `PutConfigurationSetSendingOptions`, but it has `CloudWatch.DescribeAlarms`, a binding that takes alarm resources, injects their names and grants the read at construction. A resource's props effect runs wherever the resource is first yielded, including a Lambda's cold start, where only the runtime services exist. And the SES reputation metrics exclude simulator traffic and update roughly daily on a representative volume, so they are a slow, diluted backstop, not an early warning.

## Decision

- **One alert topic per stage, owned by the stack.** `apps/backend/src/Reputation.ts` declares `AWS.SNS.Topic("Alerts")` with no props. A same-account CloudWatch alarm publishes under the topic's default policy; the gate proves it by observing a notification mail. `alchemy.run.ts` adds an `email` subscription only when `EMAILER_ALERT_EMAIL` is set. Every alarm in the stack, the three existing failure alarms and the four below, carries `AlarmActions` and `OKActions` to that topic.
- **Four reputation alarms**, declared in the same leaf module so both the stack and the dispatcher can yield them. Two on our configuration set, which gains `reputationMetricsEnabled`, at 2 percent bounces and 0.05 percent complaints. Two on the account-level metrics at AWS's suggested 5 percent and 0.1 percent, because a review or pause hits the whole account. All treat missing data as `ignore`, as AWS recommends for these metrics.
- **The dispatcher gates every slice on alarm state and enforcement status.** It binds `CloudWatch.DescribeAlarms` to the four alarms and already calls `GetAccount`. Any alarm in `ALARM`, or an `EnforcementStatus` other than `HEALTHY`, pauses the campaign with the new reason `reputation` before any claim. Nothing toggles SES; resuming is the existing `campaigns resume`, and the next slice re-evaluates.
- **Feedback is counted on the campaign.** The feedback consumer writes each event's history row and the campaign counter in one transaction: `bounced` for permanent bounces, `complained` for complaints that suppress, each conditioned on the history row not existing, so a redelivered event changes nothing. Suppression-list echoes, a bounce or complaint whose subtype says the address was already on the account list, suppress and record but do not count: nothing reached a mailbox and SES excludes them from its own rates. `campaigns get` exposes `feedback: { bounced, complained }` beside `progress`. The unused `readCampaignFeedback` is deleted.
- **Transaction conflicts are retried, lost responses are not.** The feedback consumer and the dispatcher now both write the campaign record. A DynamoDB transaction cancelled only for `TransactionConflict`, or a single-item write refused with `TransactionConflictException`, provably applied nothing, so the two conditional primitives, `updateIf` and `runTransaction`, retry those outcomes a bounded number of times before reporting `unavailable`. Their client-level opt-out from transport retries stays: an applied write with a lost response still must not be resent. The unconditional `updateRecord` keeps the client's default retries, because its callers' updates are idempotent and that policy already covers conflicts.
- **A per-run circuit breaker.** Enqueue and resume snapshot the counters as the run's baseline in the same conditional write. At every slice start the dispatcher evaluates the run's own deltas: hard bounces at or above 5 percent of accepted once 200 are accepted, or complaints at or above 0.1 percent once 1,000 are accepted, pause the campaign with reason `feedback`. Below the minimum samples one event already breaches the ratio, so the configuration-set alarm is the backstop there. Bounces from before a resume that arrive after it count against the new run; the evaluation is fresh, not exact. The thresholds are named constants, not configuration.
- **Transient bounces are a rolling window, not a state change.** Each transient or undetermined bounce adds `<receivedAt>#<feedbackId>` to a string set on `SUPPRESSION#<mailbox>/TRANSIENT`. `addressStatus` reads the three address rows in one consistent batch and reports `bouncing` when three or more entries fall inside the last thirty days; the dispatcher skips such members with that reason. No suppression is written and nothing needs undoing: the status clears itself when the window moves on.
- **Delivery delays are published and logged, not stored.** `DELIVERY_DELAY` joins the event destination and the consumer decodes it, emitting one structured log line per event with the delay type, the recipient count, the campaign id and the message id. Nothing is written: the type is the useful part, and no rule acts on a count.
- **Operators can see and clear an address.** `GET /addresses/status?email=` returns the local rows, the derived status and the account suppression entry from `GetSuppressedDestination`. `POST /addresses/unsuppress` deletes the account entry, then the local suppression and transient rows, and never touches an opt-out. Both live on `AudienceStore`, which already holds the batch read and the transaction the operations need. The CLI gains `addresses status` and `addresses unsuppress`. The address reaches SES exactly as given, trimmed but never case-changed, because the list stores entries with the case it received and its management calls require an exact match; local rows key on the lowercased mailbox regardless.

## Alternatives considered

1. **SES-side pause of the configuration set** from a guardian Lambda on the alarm. AWS's documented pattern, but Alchemy re-enables the set on the next reconcile, the binding does not exist, and the alarm stays in `ALARM` under `ignore` so the pause would not re-fire after a manual re-enable. Rejected as infrastructure and runtime fighting over one attribute.
2. **SES tenant reputation policies.** Native automatic pause per tenant, but every send must carry a tenant name and its associations, Alchemy has tenant resources but no policy resource, and the account stays shared. Rejected for now.
3. **A guardian Lambda writing a halt flag** the dispatcher reads. Correct, but one more function, rule, item and release path for what a bound `DescribeAlarms` call answers directly.
4. **Aggregating feedback at read time** from the history rows. No new binding for the feedback role, but one query per `campaigns get` and per slice, largest exactly when a campaign is bouncing.
5. **Counters on a separate item** so the two writers never collide. Removes the conflict, but the run baselines could no longer be copied in the same conditional write as the state change, and every slice and every `get` would read two items. A bounded conflict retry in the primitives is smaller and is a property every transactional writer should have.
6. **Escalating transient bounces into a written suppression.** Needs an un-suppress path and a reset rule that delivery events, which are not published, would have provided. A derived window self-heals.
7. **Counting delivery delays per campaign.** Rows, a counter and a contract field for a number no rule acts on, with the delay type dropped. Rejected by the user in favour of a typed log line.
8. **A topic access policy for CloudWatch** with source-account conditions. Cross-account hardening, not the enabling grant in the same account; its props effect would read `AWSEnvironment` at the dispatcher's cold start. Deferred until a notification is observed to fail; beta.77 has no topic-policy resource, so it would be an operator `set-topic-attributes` step.
9. **Reusing the account's existing notification topic.** No confirmation step, but a foreign resource shared with unrelated alarms and outside the stage lifecycle.
10. **Virtual Deliverability Manager.** Roughly seventy percent on top of sending cost at this volume; its advisor checks are things this project verifies itself.

## Consequences

- **IAM widens in three places.** The feedback role gains Alchemy's `TransactWriteItems` grant on the table, which is `dynamodb:ConditionCheckItem`, `DeleteItem`, `PutItem` and `UpdateItem`, beside its `PutItem`; the API role gains `ses:GetSuppressedDestination` and `ses:DeleteSuppressedDestination` on `*`; the dispatcher gains `cloudwatch:DescribeAlarms` on `*`, the resource Alchemy's binding grants because composite alarms are only returned under `*`. ADR-0003's "neither Lambda calls any SES suppression API" now holds for the feedback and dispatcher roles only.
- **One `DescribeAlarms` call joins `GetAccount` at every slice start.** Negligible at the dispatcher's cadence.
- **The account-level alarms include other senders on the account.** Halting our sends while the shared account is stressed is intended; the set-level alarms isolate our own signal.
- **`unsuppress` acts on the shared account list.** It removes the exact address given; a later hard bounce re-adds it. The command is an operator tool, not a customer flow.
- **An alarm test leaves state behind.** Under `ignore` a forced `ALARM` persists until reset to `OK`; the gate resets it, and the README says so.
- **A fresh stage mails up to five `OK:` notifications on its first deploy.** Alarms start in `INSUFFICIENT_DATA` and `OKActions` fire on the transition to `OK` from any state; the three failure alarms treat missing data as not breaching and the two account-level alarms have data from the shared account. The OK actions stay, because an alarm returning to OK is the signal to resume a paused campaign; the README names the burst.
- **The email subscription needs one confirmation per stage** and SNS deletes an unconfirmed one after 48 hours; a later deploy re-subscribes. Alchemy's `pendingConfirmation` output is not reliable for email and is not used.
- **Simulator traffic cannot move the reputation metrics or the account list.** The alarm path is proven with `SetAlarmState`, the breaker with labelled bounce addresses, the un-suppress path with an entry the test seeds, and the transient window and delay logging with unit tests only: the simulator has no soft-bounce or delay address, and its bounces are not added to the account suppression list.
- **Gmail complaints stay invisible.** Gmail sends no feedback loop to SES; its spam rate lives in Postmaster Tools and is a manual weekly duty, together with the DMARC aggregate reports arriving at the operator mailbox.
- **A `feedback` pause is a list problem, not a transient.** Resuming without cleaning the list trips the breaker again after the next minimum sample; the baseline makes that a fresh evaluation rather than an instant re-trip.
- **`bouncing` is derived, so it expires.** An address that recovers is mailable again thirty days after its third transient bounce without anyone acting; an address that keeps bouncing keeps being skipped.
- **The breaker is fast and the alarms are slow.** The breaker reacts within a slice of the feedback arriving; the reputation metrics move about daily. The README names which is which.
- **Resource props must stay runtime-safe.** The dispatcher yields the alarms, which yield the topic; anything in those props runs at its cold start. The topic has no props and the alarms read only outputs.
- **Schema changes are free.** No stage holds production data, so the campaign record and the feedback rows change shape without migration.

## Confirmation

The plan's gate deploys an ephemeral stage with the alert address set to the operator mailbox, confirms the subscription from the received mail, then: forces one reputation alarm to `ALARM`, confirms the state, and observes a queued campaign pause with reason `reputation` and a notification mail; resets it and resumes to completion; runs a campaign to 400 labelled simulator addresses of which half bounce and observes the pause with reason `feedback` after at least 200 accepted, with `feedback.bounced` converging on the number of bounce recipients accepted; seeds one address on the account suppression list, shows and clears it through the CLI, and observes the entry gone. Unit suites prove the transient window, the delay log line, the breaker boundaries, the guard mapping and the conflict retry.

Observed 2026-09-15 on ephemeral stage `test-d`: SNS confirmation mail received and `ConfirmSubscription` succeeded; `ALARM:` and `OK:` mails for `SetBounceRate` arrived at the operator mailbox (default topic policy was enough); gate campaign paused `reputation` and completed after resume; 400-member breaker paused `feedback`; `addresses status` / `unsuppress` cleared a seeded `example.com` account-list entry (SES `PutSuppressedDestination` rejects mailbox-simulator addresses). Stage destroyed after the run.
